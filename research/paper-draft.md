# When a Critic Stops the Generator: Studying Jev Guidance in Frozen Granite

Working title and manuscript scaffold, 2026-09-21. Not submitted, peer reviewed,
or a report of a successful replacement architecture. Authors, affiliations,
contributions and publication venue are intentionally unset pending owner decisions.

## Abstract draft

We study whether a hosted, non-generative judgment model can improve answers from
a frozen small language model through intermediate inference-time guidance. Our
initial fixed-choice pipeline returned Jev's final classification, so it could
not establish improved Granite-generated answers. We preserve that result and
evaluate a corrected three-arm design in which Granite generates every final
answer. Across 200 GSM8K and 200 ProofWriter problems, three seeds and three
conditions, all 3,600 planned outcomes are retained and their final-token provenance
is audited. Intermediate Jev selection scores 56.5% on math versus 61.5% for
single-candidate generation and 67.8% for likelihood selection; logic scores are
52.5%, 52.7% and 52.8%, respectively. The adjusted math difference against likelihood
selection is −11.33 percentage points, with interval [−18.17, −4.50]. A subsequent
offline analysis separates first-step scorer rejection in 307/600 guided logic
runs from no valid proposal in 265/600. These results expose interacting proposal,
verification and stopping-policy limitations; they do not establish a causal
benefit from any alternative insertion point. We specify a gated investigation
of local critic quality and bounded output-logit intervention as future work.

## 1. Question and scope

Can a second model improve the final answers *generated by* a small frozen model
when it participates during inference? Distinguish generated-answer quality from
classification quality, judged trace quality and mechanism correctness. A critic
can select a label without generating tokens; this does not make its label a
Granite-generated answer. The original objective requires Granite to remain the
answerer. A formal distinction between generator and judge is part of the method.

The system uses original IBM Granite 4.0 1B, pinned revision
`6a7381ba1f54d684ff508d991aeb7dc580157103`, and hosted Jev `jev-1.13.0`.
Despite a hybrid-family implementation name, the configuration contains 40
attention layers and no experts. No model weights were trained or changed.
See [architecture audit](architecture-reassessment.md) for exact source evidence.

## 3. Development history and correction

The [study register](study-register.md) preserves eleven historical reports,
including smoke checks, failed step control, proposal-prompt development, a
fixed-choice workaround, the larger final-classifier study, and two corrected
generation pilots. This chronology is a development record, not eleven independent
replications of a single unchanged hypothesis.

In the earlier 2,400-job study, guided reasoning followed by Jev Choice scored
507/600 versus direct Jev's 508/600. In 542/600 guided cases no intermediate step
survived. The observed quality was largely a final-classifier outcome. A conflicting
label-only versus label-and-reason contract further limits generated-answer
comparisons derived from those paths. These mistakes should be reported explicitly,
with original artifacts retained, not presented as positive evidence for generation.

The replacement's first 48-job development pilot failed its format criterion.
After a common EOS/label/prompt correction, the same development cases passed.
Admission tested format, exact token ownership and exercised guidance, not critic
discrimination. The subsequent main experiment was frozen before test inference.

## 4. Corrected study method

Three conditions use the same staged prompt and final-generation rule: a single
sampled intermediate candidate; three candidates selected by Granite mean log
probability; or three candidates selected by Jev support/progress with both gates
at 0.5. Every arm generates its final answer greedily with Granite, including
when no intermediate proposal survives. Jev scores only intermediate frames.

The sample comprises 200 distinct GSM8K test problems and 200 distinct ProofWriter
OWA D5 test theories, one question per theory, with three seeds (42, 43, 44).
Logic labels are balanced 67 TRUE, 67 FALSE and 66 UNKNOWN; derivable questions
are stratified over depths 0–5. Prior exposed logic theories and new development
cases were excluded. All model inputs omit reference answers. Independent
grading uses numeric matching or exact canonical logic labels. Incorrect,
incomplete, invalid and missing outcomes stay in the planned denominator.

This is a staged-generation study, not a measurement of unrestricted default
Granite chat. Fixed ceilings are eight 64-token reasoning steps, three proposals
where applicable, 96 reserved final tokens, 1,632 padded decode slots, 100,000
prefill tokens, 768 path tokens, 4,096 context tokens, eight Jev calls and 90 seconds,
including a 15-second final reserve. Actual computation differs by policy.
Full method/source/data hashes are in the [frozen protocol](../docs/generated-answer-experiment.md)
and [final report](../reports/2026-09-20-generated-answer-study/README.md).

Execution used one L40S, BF16, Transformers 4.57.1, Torch 2.8.0+cu128 and
Python 3.12.13. There were no provider/backend failures or unknown paid usage.
Loading and warm-up are excluded from controller timings. This measures a
remote Jev service; network and provider compute were not separately identified.

## 5. Results

| Task | Single Granite | Likelihood Granite | Intermediate Jev + Granite |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Math | 369/600 · 61.50% | 407/600 · 67.83% | 339/600 · 56.50% |
| Logic | 316/600 · 52.67% | 317/600 · 52.83% | 315/600 · 52.50% |

| Prespecified contrast | Difference, pp | Adjusted interval, pp |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Math: Jev − single | −5.00 | [−12.00, +1.83] |
| Math: Jev − likelihood | −11.33 | [−18.17, −4.50] |
| Logic: Jev − single | −0.17 | [−4.83, +4.17] |
| Logic: Jev − likelihood | −0.33 | [−5.33, +4.17] |

Intervals are 98.75% paired problem-cluster bootstrap intervals with 5,000 draws,
Bonferroni-adjusted for four prespecified contrasts. Three seeds do not turn
200 problems into 600 independent observations. The broad prespecified success
criterion was not met. Intervals including zero do not establish equivalence.

All 3,600 final generations passed the token audit, reproduced locally from the
retrieved traces. All 1,200 paired initial likelihood/Jev candidate batches matched.
Mean math controller times were 1.97 s single, 2.47 s likelihood and 2.87 s Jev;
logic was 0.86 s, 1.28 s and 0.62 s. Faster guided logic reflects early termination
and less work, not an equal-work speedup. Guided runs used 2,254 Jev API calls.

## 6. Exploratory failure analysis

The [new offline report](../reports/2026-09-21-architecture-reassessment/README.md)
replays recorded parsing and eligibility without new model calls or regrading
final answers. In logic, first-step acceptance occurred in 28/600 guided runs;
307 stopped because all valid options failed the gates, and 265 had no valid
option to score. Among 808 unique scored candidates over all guided logic paths,
614 failed only progress, 132 failed both gates, one failed only support and
61 passed. Among 334 all-rejected batches, 313 contained a support-passing option
blocked by progress.

These are judgments and control-flow facts. They do not prove the accepted or
rejected candidates are semantically correct, nor identify the counterfactual
final answers under another stopping policy. The policy was designed to reject
restatements, so some progress rejections may be correct yet still leave the
generator without a usable continuation. Candidate availability and scorer quality
must be evaluated separately. The pilot already showed poor retained-step coverage;
future admission needs a stronger gate than correct formatting alone.

## 7. Proposed integration, not an experimental result

The [new architecture](architecture-reassessment.md) specifies sparse, bounded
logit intervention after Granite's final head/scaling, informed by Jev judgments
of local counterfactual text. It removes the automatic relationship between low
progress and forcing a final answer. Its first prerequisite is independent critic
discrimination on generated candidates. A real internal-layer alternative requires
a validated mapping from judgments to activation directions and a causal site
study; no best layer is currently known.

Do not insert a positive-results table for this design until the
[next-study gates](next-experiment.md) are executed. If future work improves only
semantic grounding, describe that domain and retain the negative math/logic record.

## 8. Limitations and threats to validity

- Jev both ranks and rejects steps; the main comparison does not separate those
  effects. Prompt staging is common across arms but has no default-chat control.
- Equal configured limits did not produce equal actual compute. More search and
  different stopping are potential explanations independent of scorer knowledge.
- Final correctness does not certify intermediate explanations. Most trace
  diagnostics are mechanical, and current qualitative readings are not blinded
  independent human annotations.
- Shared test benchmarks may have appeared in pretraining. Excluding previously
  used cases in this project does not establish training-data noncontamination.
- A small model, one checkpoint, two tasks and a hosted judge version limit
  generalization. Provider outputs can vary despite pinned requested identifiers.
- Private external traces limit public per-example reproduction. Rights-cleared
  authored future fixtures can improve transparency but introduce template bias.
- Post-hoc inspection informed the redesign. The old test is exposed and cannot
  test the new hypothesis independently. Smaller or negative effects are reportable.
