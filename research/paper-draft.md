# From Claim Verification to Learned Representation Bridges: Jev Guidance in Granite

Working title and manuscript scaffold, updated 2026-09-24. Not submitted, peer reviewed,
or a report of a successful replacement architecture. Authors, affiliations,
contributions and publication venue are intentionally unset pending owner decisions.

## Abstract draft

We investigate whether hosted typed Jev judgments improve answers generated by
frozen IBM Granite 4.0 1B, preserving an initial attribution error, negative
step/logit-guidance studies and subsequent attention interventions. R18 establishes
conditional assistance before layer 19 within one native prefill, with unchanged
weights and full-vocabulary token ownership. Its routing evidence remains mixed.

R19 separates predicted treatment benefit from evidence sufficiency and uses
source or existing-instruction attention. On 608 fresh test inputs, native/dual
scores are 26.74%/43.06% authored parser accuracy, 27.68%/32.95% Hotpot answer F1,
and 25.44%/33.10% adapted SQuAD F1. The primary authored dual-minus-relevance
contrast is +15.28 percentage points [9.38,21.88], using a 99.1667% cluster interval.
A benefit gate saves 54.44% of requests, but all three primary within-domain
routing intervals include zero. Static instruction steering without Jev produces
46.53%/32.90%/34.49%, and no domain establishes dual superiority over that control.

Individual-answer inspection exposes material measurement artifacts: equivalent
abstentions receive different parser scores, and lexical F1 can favor brevity or
incorrect partial overlap. These examples limit semantic interpretation of the
positive score effects. Complete audits and public replay account for 7,312 R19
generations, 139,811 final tokens and 1,068 successful provider receipts, with
unchanged model weights and explicit implementation/audit corrections. The record
supports working internal control mechanisms and bounded score effects; it does
not establish reliable semantic improvement, cross-domain call selection or a
generally superior LLM architecture.

R20 completes a blinded semantic-score study on 528 fresh inputs. Qwen3-14B passes
96/96 constructed validation cases, then grades 1,584 Granite outputs as 1,070
anonymous unique packets. Native/dual scores are 30.21%/42.71% authored,
74.17%/73.33% Hotpot and 61.67%/67.50% SQuAD. Only the authored primary contrast,
+12.50 pp [4.17,21.18], excludes zero; no static-control superiority is established.
A registered 24-packet blind inspection agrees on 21 labels and reveals two clear
answer-completeness failures plus one annotation ambiguity. Further examples
expose citation-only answers credited as correct. These errors prevent treating
the positive judge-score effect as reliable semantic improvement. R20 preserves
all primary grades and identifies evaluator transfer as a remaining measurement
problem before architectural optimization.

R21 narrows verification to local assignment claims with structural ground truth.
An initial 192-world admission lacks sufficient natural errors; a fixed fresh
384-world replication passes, correctly judging all 383 assessed drafts including
36 incorrect drafts, while one partial name remains unassessed. R22 then trains
a 65,568-parameter feedback-conditioned residual bridge after Granite block 19.
On 384 fresh test worlds, native accuracy is 90.89%, while both live-feedback and
matched constant-feedback adapters average 92.19% over two seeds. The primary
live-minus-native contrast is +1.30 pp [−1.30,4.04]; live-minus-constant is zero.
Shuffled and oracle feedback reproduce every live final token sequence. Thus the
learned bridge is operational, but no added benefit from Jev is observed. Original
model weights remain unchanged; all adapter checkpoints and raw evidence are
retained. These narrow results do not establish a novel superior architecture.

R23 establishes a ten-task evaluation contract and completes a four-domain public
development baseline: native 1B/newer thinking 3B score 19/24 vs 21/24 math,
2/12 vs 7/12 IFBench, and—under a separately recorded post-hoc choice readout—
12/28 vs 18/28 MMLU-Pro and 6/12 vs 4/12 MuSR. The frozen primary scores are
preserved. Different operating profiles, 13 larger-model length stops, exposed
development cases and related stories limit these comparisons. R24 then finds
that Jev flags 21/23 reference-wrong native answers, with 3/37 false flags on
reference-correct answers. All original answers remain unchanged. This identifies
a candidate feedback signal for conditional repair, not a beneficial architecture,
a ten-benchmark win or a claim that the small generator surpasses larger models.

R25 trains a 262,144-parameter Jev-gated repair branch on natural public math and
science drafts, with matched constant training and feedback ablations. On 192
fresh pilot cases, full repair lowers native 59.38% to 44.79% (−14.58 pp,
individual 95% interval [−21.61,−7.29]). A retention policy registered before test
instead reaches 66.67%: science rises 71.88% to 86.46%, while math stays 46.88%.
All retained science recoveries change parseable wrong choices to correct choices;
no correct native answer is damaged under retention in either seed. Full repair
introduces extensive formatting failures, and 34/96 native math outputs are
unparseable. The selective science result is promising but is offline replay,
not measured execution savings or broad/larger-model superiority. All raw evidence,
12 adapter checkpoints, numerical/service interruptions and exact public replay
are retained; no primary score is retrospectively regraded.

R26-A measures actual selective execution on 50 exposed development cases: 20
repairs and 30 retained drafts per arm, with no reduction in Jev requests. Three
native model profiles complete, but a failed output-readout contract prevents
fresh benchmark admission. Exact-token audits and replay pass; measured runtime
exposes substantial full-suite cost uncertainty. These results support further
engineering and evaluation work, not broad model superiority.

## 1. Question and scope

Can a second model improve the final answers *generated by* a small frozen model
when it participates during inference? Distinguish generated-answer quality from
classification quality, judged trace quality and mechanism correctness. A critic
can select a label without generating tokens; this does not make its label a
Granite-generated answer. The original objective requires Granite to remain the
answerer. A formal distinction between generator and judge is part of the method.

The system uses original IBM Granite 4.0 1B, pinned revision
`6a7381ba1f54d684ff508d991aeb7dc580157103`, and hosted Jev `jev-1.13.0`.
Despite a hybrid-family implementation name, the configuration contains 40
attention layers and no experts. Original Granite and Jev weights were not trained
or changed. R19 fits a separate small regression controller from development outcomes;
R22 and R25 train new residual adapter weights inside Granite's computation.
See [architecture audit](architecture-reassessment.md) for exact source evidence.

## 2. Related work

Position the work against FUDGE, Controlled Decoding, DeAL and other inference-time
reward-guided sampling; separate output-logit methods from PPLM and calibrated
activation intervention. Compare step-critic requirements to process-supervision
research. The [source ledger](related-work.md) includes recent 2026 screening and
explicit review depth. The [attention-method review](adaptive-attention-related-work.md)
also compares PASTA, AutoPASTA, Spotlight and CAFE with R16, documenting
existing automatic/dynamic attention steering and unimplemented comparators.
The [boundary-attention review](boundary-attention-related-work.md) compares
attention-based monitoring and conditional assistance with Lookback Lens, RAUQ,
SeaKR, CtrlA and DSSP-RAG. None is an implemented baseline in this study. Complete
remaining method comparisons and empirical controls before making a novelty or
superiority claim. None of their reported gains is a result of this project.
The [R19 comparison](benefit-sufficiency-related-work.md) adds RouteLLM's learned
relative-quality routing and separates that established principle from R19's
specific callback and source/instruction attention mechanism.
The [R22 prior-art check](learned-feedback-related-work.md) includes representation
tuning (ReFT), conditional modulation (FiLM), EchoTrust's verifier-conditioned
retry adapters, and hidden-state verification for diffusion generation. R22's
external scalar feedback and bounded residual are a particular integration of
established principles. None of those methods was implemented as a comparator;
using an internal adapter alone establishes neither novelty nor superiority.

## 4. Corrected study method

Three conditions use the same staged prompt and final-generation rule: a single
sampled intermediate candidate; three candidates selected by Granite mean log
probability; or three candidates selected by Jev support/progress with both gates
at 0.5. Every arm generates its final answer greedily with Granite, including
when no intermediate proposal survives. Jev scores only intermediate frames.

The sample comprises 200 distinct GSM8K test problems and 200 distinct ProofWriter
OWA D5 test theories, one question per theory, with three seeds (42, 43, 44).
Logic labels are balanced 67 TRUE, 67 FALSE and 66 UNKNOWN; derivable questions
are stratified over depths 0–5. Prior exposed logic theories and new development
cases were excluded. All model inputs omit reference answers. Independent
grading uses numeric matching or exact canonical logic labels. Incorrect,
incomplete, invalid and missing outcomes stay in the planned denominator.

This is a staged-generation study, not a measurement of unrestricted default
Granite chat. Fixed ceilings are eight 64-token reasoning steps, three proposals
where applicable, 96 reserved final tokens, 1,632 padded decode slots, 100,000
prefill tokens, 768 path tokens, 4,096 context tokens, eight Jev calls and 90 seconds,
including a 15-second final reserve. Actual computation differs by policy.
Full method/source/data hashes are in the [frozen protocol](../docs/generated-answer-experiment.md)
and [final report](../reports/2026-09-20-generated-answer-study/README.md).

Execution used one L40S, BF16, Transformers 4.57.1, Torch 2.8.0+cu128 and
Python 3.12.13. There were no provider/backend failures or unknown paid usage.
Loading and warm-up are excluded from controller timings. This measures a
remote Jev service; network and provider compute were not separately identified.

## 6. Exploratory failure analysis

The [new offline report](../reports/2026-09-21-architecture-reassessment/README.md)
replays recorded parsing and eligibility without new model calls or regrading
final answers. In logic, first-step acceptance occurred in 28/600 guided runs;
307 stopped because all valid options failed the gates, and 265 had no valid
option to score. Among 808 unique scored candidates over all guided logic paths,
614 failed only progress, 132 failed both gates, one failed only support and
61 passed. Among 334 all-rejected batches, 313 contained a support-passing option
blocked by progress.

These are judgments and control-flow facts. They do not prove the accepted or
rejected candidates are semantically correct, nor identify the counterfactual
final answers under another stopping policy. The policy was designed to reject
restatements, so some progress rejections may be correct yet still leave the
generator without a usable continuation. Candidate availability and scorer quality
must be evaluated separately. The pilot already showed poor retained-step coverage;
future admission needs a stronger gate than correct formatting alone.

## 7. Bounded logit prototype and incomplete critic admission

The [new architecture](architecture-reassessment.md) specifies sparse, bounded
logit intervention after Granite's final head/scaling, informed by Jev judgments
of local counterfactual text. It removes the automatic relationship between low
progress and forcing a final answer. Its first prerequisite is independent critic
discrimination on generated candidates. A real internal-layer alternative requires
a validated mapping from judgments to activation directions and a causal site
study; no best layer is currently known.

The [development report](../reports/2026-09-21-logit-guidance/README.md) now records
versioned inference on 60 authored worlds. V1 sampled three complete claims;
only two scored sets contained both independently graded correct and incorrect
claims. On 59 unique graded/scored candidates Jev separated all 17 false from
42 true claims at support thresholds 0.2/0.8, but this favorable conditional subset
excluded unassessed text and did not pass an independent critic gate.

A development-only revision branches at a Granite-generated subject/copula
boundary, taking four distinct next-token actions with short greedy continuations.
Jev's rubric remains unchanged; the exact grader adds one positive established
wrapper. The seventh world's request returned HTTP 400 without a usage receipt;
no paid retry followed. Separate local completion of only the 53 never-attempted
proposal jobs yields 56/60 checkpoints, 224 proposals, 150 independently graded
claims (67.0%), 54 worlds with a graded correct option, and eight fully graded
mixed-quality sets. These are development opportunity counts, not critic accuracy
or new final-answer results. Most graded claims concern what is not established.

The actual returned-logit controller was mechanically checked on the four exact
prefixes with successful pre-error Jev scores. Across native, zero, Jev, shuffled
and synthetic controls, no-op paths matched and full model state hashes were
unchanged. Jev changed all four distributions within the 0.02 KL ceiling (observed
maximum 0.013683), changing 6/256 fixed root draws. Those draws are not independent
problems. The four selected continuation seeds yielded identical paths across all
five modes. All 20 final continuations were Granite-generated, but none closed the
requested final frame. Thus probability/provenance correctness and final-format
reliability differ. Scores were replayed without fresh hosted requests; the assembled
live checkpoint function has offline tests but no new paid end-to-end trial.

The 100-world critic gate and Gate C remain unexecuted. Independent proposal/grader
coverage, provider failure reconciliation and a reliable final contract remain
necessary before a new prospective test. No positive answer-quality table is
justified for this prototype. Future success in a narrow semantic domain would
not erase the previous negative math/logic findings.

### 7.1 R13: complete comparison after operational repair

The owner requested resolving the remaining issues and executing a complete
comparison under the existing budget. This changed admission prospectively:
R13 requires operational correctness, not a positive pilot accuracy gain or a
retroactively claimed R12 critic-gate pass. The [protocol](structured-study-protocol.md)
and [V2 correction](structured-study-v2-protocol.md) retain both decisions.

Two frozen API probes succeeded. The earlier unknown-usage call stayed charged
at its full reservation maximum, and diagnostic error bodies are now retained
with credential redaction. The first 168-job L40S pilot completed all jobs and
192 authenticated Jev calls, but only 121 finals obeyed the unconstrained label
instruction. All 960 claim grades and token/control audits passed. V2 applies
the same three-label final grammar to every arm; original Granite logits select
the label, and no Jev final classification occurs. Its 168-job development pilot
passed all operational gates and independent token reconstruction, with all
96 first-candidate-pool comparisons identical. This enforces syntax rather than
teaching the model to follow formatting instructions.

The full frozen test has 300 authored worlds, 100 per final label, three seeds
and seven arms (6,300 jobs): direct Granite, staged native sampling, likelihood
root search, bounded Jev root guidance, shuffled scores, zero bias with shadow
work, and whole-lookahead commitment under the same root policy. All arms use
the same prompt and final-label grammar. Six logical motifs, distractor entities,
and longer test chains (3/5 versus development 2/4) permit exact independent
forward-closure grading. This remains a narrow synthetic distribution; the
motifs overlap across splits. Test labels and symbolic internals are withheld
from model/scorer inputs. It is separate from R10's external benchmark results.

Staged arms produce two grammar-constrained atomic claims. At the first property
boundary in each, four distinct next-token roots receive greedy lookaheads. Jev
judges complete local assertions against the original evidence. Its bounded
reweighting commits one root and returns to native sampling; it does not reject
all candidates or stop reasoning based on a progress threshold. The reference
for the 0.02 KL ceiling is the common grammar-conditioned distribution, not
unrestricted Granite. The [method comparison](decoding-method-comparison.md)
distinguishes this support heuristic from a learned future-answer value function.

The soft-step arm changes both commitment length and continuation policy: a
selected evaluated root carries its complete greedy lookahead, whereas the
one-root arm samples the remaining tail. It therefore compares these two
specified policies; an observed difference cannot isolate an abstract insertion
point independently of tail decoding. Direct/staged likewise tests the whole
addition of constrained claims, not hidden reasoning ability in isolation.

Primary contrasts are Jev minus direct, staged and likelihood, using 5,000
world-cluster bootstrap draws after averaging the three seeds within each world.
Individual 98.333% intervals adjust for those three comparisons; a prespecified
useful gain requires at least five percentage points and an interval above zero.
Other ablations and critic/accepted-claim diagnostics are descriptive. Failed,
invalid and missing outcomes remain in denominators. No test outcome will alter
the controller. The [report](../reports/2026-09-21-structured-study/README.md)
contains both pilots, both test segments and the completed comparison below.

### 7.3 R14: completed attention-to-evidence study

The [prospective R14 protocol](evidence-attention-protocol.md) evaluates Jev as a
source-span relevance judge. Scores become bounded evidence-key biases inside
selected Granite attention heads before softmax/value aggregation, for query
positions after the evidence block. A relevance map is computed once before final
generation and remains static. Jev sees the question and complete source records,
without final choices, reference answers or hidden tensors. This is external
semantic control of internal attention; [PASTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02262)
is the established attention-steering/head-profiling precedent.

Development profiles all 640 query heads individually at ln(4) on 24 worlds, then
compares the top 1/2/4/8 heads at ln(2)/ln(4)/ln(8) on 72 other worlds in two context
conditions. Oracle annotations serve only development selection and the named
privileged diagnostic arm. The selected eight heads at ln(8) raise calibration
accuracy from 46.53% to 67.36%, with no clean-context harm, passing the predeclared
causal-headroom gate. A 12-context Jev pilot passes operational admission before
the frozen test; a positive pilot effect is not required. All development attempts
remain public, including unfavorable head/configuration interventions.

Selected zero-based (layer, query head) pairs are (34,4), (38,11), (37,14), (30,4),
(23,8), (19,6), (21,13), (19,11). Bias is ln(8) × max(0,2r−1), with all-equal
scores producing no-op behavior and ordinary BF16 mask quantization. The hook
preserves causal masking, RoPE, GQA and weights, and is removed per request even
on errors. Cached/incremental inputs are rejected in this serial reference.

The test has 360 fresh authored containment worlds, balanced across depths 1/2/3
and answerable/missing final links. Each has one or six unrelated three-link chains.
Test wording reverses the development relation template. A separate visible-text
parser reconstructs edge direction and follows the queried chain to independently
grade the answer. All arms expose all six color labels and UNKNOWN; Granite chooses
one token from the same constrained action space. No intermediate text is forced.
The final is generator-owned and attention-guided, unlike R13's unassisted final.

minus lexical +2.36 [−0.97,5.97]; minus prompt +4.44 [−0.28,9.17]. The conjunctive

controls, broadly useful accuracy, or the best insertion point across tasks.

### 7.4 R15: deployed-score calibration and fresh attention refinement

The [prospective R15 protocol](evidence-attention-v2-protocol.md) searches 90
configurations over top 1/2/4/8/12 previously ranked heads, ln(4)/ln(8)/ln(16),
soft/hard50/hard80 relevance maps, and all post-evidence versus final query scope.
Ninety-six new development worlds, each with two contexts, select once under
predeclared class/context harm floors. The chosen twelve-head, ln(16), hard50
configuration keeps the original query scope and acts across nine attention layers.
Development accuracy is 127/192 (66.15%), versus 74 native and 86 previous Jev;
these optimistic selection outcomes are separate from the test below.

All new development/test/challenge aliases exclude exposed R14 and each other.
Both new development and test use the containment wording previously reserved for
R14 test; the original policy is rerun on the same new instances. Every final
answer is one Granite-generated token from six colors plus UNKNOWN. Jev receives
only the question and source records, and returns typed source relevance once
before each forward set; no reference labels, hidden tensors or final choices
are supplied to the judge. Model weights remain unchanged.

### 7.6 R17: selective dispatch, timing and evidence-mass conservation

The finite grid combines additive versus evidence-mass-preserving steering,

The conserving operation subtracts a log-partition correction on all evidence

objective. No final answer or reference is supplied to Jev. Granite chooses all
final tokens from its full vocabulary, and no weights are changed.

### 7.7 R18: a conditional request inside one Granite prefill

R17's pilot gate can discard eight generated tokens and restart the entire prompt
when it requests Jev. R18 implements the [prospective boundary design](boundary-attention-plan.md):
observe native attention at layer 18, decide immediately before zero-indexed layer
19, and continue the same prefill/cache on either a call or skip. The unchanged
additive/all-token strength-5 treatment starts at that layer and controls eleven
query heads across nine layers. Jev scores only the original question and source
text; it receives no hidden features, references or final answer. Granite still
owns all full-vocabulary final tokens. No neural weights are trained.

The observer reconstructs one post-RoPE last-prompt-query attention row. Per-head
source mass supplies three features: total evidence mass, entropy of the mean
within-evidence source distribution, and Jensen–Shannon disagreement among heads,
with entropy quantities normalized by log(source count). One-source inputs have
zero source entropy and disagreement. Saved per-head masses support reconstruction;
features precede any intervention. The callback bridges a serial model worker to
the async provider loop, retaining native lower-layer state during the wait. A
failed receipt preserves the native branch and the charged error. There is no
output pilot, discarded token, new retrieval, cache rollback or second prefill in
the boundary arms. The [method](../reports/2026-09-22-boundary-attention/method.md)
contains the layer diagram, formulas and ownership limitations.

Development comprises 108 fresh authored heavy contexts, 72 Hotpot questions and
80 SQuAD2 questions, balanced answerable/impossible for SQuAD. Native and always
guided boundary branches provide 520 outcomes. Threshold candidates span three
features, both directions and 21 empirical quantiles, plus never-calling. Maximize
equal-domain quality under an unweighted 50% development request ceiling, then
prefer fewer requests and canonical JSON. Fit the R17 pilot-feature rule separately.
The selected boundary rule calls when normalized head disagreement is at most
0.05478179526607767 (118/260 development inputs, 45.38%); the selected pilot rule
calls when copy fraction is at most 0.8571428571428571 (128/260, 49.23%). These
are observed development choices, not evidence that low disagreement generally
means uncertainty or that confident answers should receive help.

Test comprises 252 authored worlds in light/heavy contexts (504 inputs), 240
Hotpot questions and 240 SQuAD2 questions (120 answerable, 120 impossible). Nine
arms yield 8,856 test outcomes: native, always guided, boundary-never,
boundary-always, selected boundary gate, random boundary gate, selected pilot
gate, lexical scores and shuffled Jev scores. The selected boundary gate executes
first on every input; all other arms run in fixed randomized order and reuse exact
receipts. Physical paid attempts and standalone logical calls are distinct counts.
Native/boundary-never and always/boundary-always isolate observation overhead;
conditional token-path identities verify branches without constituting independent
replications. All generations use a common open-explicit prompt and 32-token cap.

Authored names exclude previous study exposure. All 315 new Hotpot development,
admission and test questions exclude 466 earlier R16/R17 questions, retain ten
complete paragraphs, and meet the 3,072-token prompt ceiling. SQuAD is new to the
project: the public development file is split by article into development,
admission and test pools before selection, with one question per paragraph and
complete original paragraphs split into sentence source units. This is not an
assessment on the official hidden test, and pretraining exposure is unknown.

The SQuAD contract maps a frozen conservative whole-answer uncertainty phrase to
empty prediction, then takes maximum normalized EM/F1 over references. Empty/EOS
alone scores zero under this adaptation. Raw unmodified-answer EM/F1 are also
retained. Thus, the study permits natural uncertainty without forcing UNKNOWN,
but the adapted metric is not the official SQuAD no-answer-probability protocol.
Authored parser accuracy and Hotpot whole-answer F1 retain their prior definitions.

The three primary comparisons measure gate quality over expected random guidance
at the same actual test call count, separately by domain. This is the covariance
between the binary call and guided-minus-native quality; it measures selection
value, not merely fewer calls. Ten thousand bootstrap draws use authored worlds,
Hotpot questions and SQuAD articles as clusters. Individual 98.333% intervals give
nominal 95% family coverage. Other contrasts use exploratory 95% intervals, and
the 3 pp loss margin versus always guidance is a separate descriptive question.
No all-controls success conjunction or post-test budget search is applied.

Real-checkpoint admission passes 27/27 comparisons across nine fixtures with
maximum full-logit and all-layer KV differences both exactly 0.0, below the frozen
1e-4 tolerance. Native, always-guided and forced-failed-receipt paths retain the
same tokens/cache lengths and one-prefill work. Admission uses canned scores,
not a paid provider check. The subsequent live study retains actual receipts,
failures, forward/layer-token work, first-token times and peak allocated GPU memory.

Attention-based monitoring and conditional assistance have substantial prior art.
[Lookback Lens](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.84.pdf) selects generated
chunks using attention-based classifiers; [RAUQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20045v1)
computes recurrent attention-based uncertainty over generated tokens;
[SeaKR](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1312.pdf) uses sampled internal-state
consistency for retrieval; [CtrlA](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.652.pdf)
combines representation steering and confidence monitoring; and
[DSSP-RAG](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.549.pdf) trains mixed attention
for conditional knowledge integration. The [method comparison](boundary-attention-related-work.md)
distinguishes R18's particular callback, feature and treatment combination. These
methods were not implemented as live controls; R18 establishes no superiority over
them or historical novelty for the general principles.

### Formatting confounds and descriptive critic agreement

unknown. Keeping highly rated native answers avoids those observed damages.

## 8. Limitations and threats to validity

- R25 is a 192-question two-domain pilot with two fitted seeds and individual,
  rather than simultaneous, 95% intervals. Format failures materially affect
  native math and science repairs. The fixed endpoint is retained; qualitative
  inspection is post-hoc and unblinded. The predeclared retention gain is an
  offline output-policy result, with no measured skipped model execution or API
  savings. Both retained constant and retained blind policies use Jev for routing.
  Selected checkpoints differ between live and equally trained constant modes;
  same-weight feedback ablations help assess sensitivity but do not establish a
  general benefit from the hosted critic. Eight missing training judgments use
  explicit neutral fallback; no test score is missing. The fixed layer, rank,
  optimizer and scalar final-correctness feedback were not proven optimal.
  No fresh replication of the retention gain, full ten-task evaluation or R25
  larger-model comparison was run. Public replay verifies computation, not an
  independent experimental replication.

- R23 uses 76 public development problems and different native-oriented 1B/3B
  inference profiles, not official full-benchmark runs or matched computation.
  Some source-test cases are explicitly consumed as development. The additional
  whole-option readout follows native-output inspection; neither it nor the
  primary parser is independently annotated semantic accuracy. Token-limit
  outcomes must remain visible when interpreting larger-model scores.
- R24 measures a critic on 60 exposed native answers, selected for unambiguous
  readout. It excludes four unresolved answers and twelve exact-verifier prompts.
  Its strong pooled discrimination has small domain denominators and narrative
  false alarms; pretraining exposure is unknown. No answer is repaired, no
  feedback-conditioned hidden layer is tested, and every eligible example
  receives a Jev call. The result establishes neither generated-answer gains
  nor sparse dispatch savings, transfer or architectural novelty.

- R21/R22 share narrow authored logical templates; fresh identities and assignments
  are not broad task transfer. The R21 replication was added after insufficient
  initial error exposure, and its partial-name case exposes an oracle-coverage
  limitation. R22 trains on authored drafts rather than natural model errors,
  tests one layer/rank/learning rate and two seeds, and starts with high native
  accuracy. Correct support judgments do not supply a corrective explanation.
  Verifier requests include randomly ordered, unlabeled constructed true/false
  intermediate claims alongside the model draft. Cross-question cues are possible;
  standalone draft-only verification was not measured. No final badge facts or
  reference enter Jev. The matched constant adapter has the same nominal parameter
  count, but its 32 conditioning weights multiply zero and cannot influence output.
  The constant control can learn the final task without using feedback; zero
  answer changes under permutation/oracle feedback do not prove zero activation
  changes. Primary inference preserves a narrow complete-color contract, while
  the secondary descriptive grammar was registered after test drafts started.
  Neither this operational bridge nor its inconclusive native gain establishes
  general semantic improvement, architecture novelty or deployment efficiency.

- R20's independent automated judge passes simple constructed templates but
  demonstrably supplies missing candidate facts from the evidence on generated
  answers. Citation-only acceptance is asymmetric across arms, and upstream
  reference/answerability inconsistencies remain. Its score effects are not
  verified semantic gains. The 24-packet review is small, by unique packet, and
  performed by the implementing coding assistant; it provides no independent
  human annotation or precise error-rate estimate. Six primary intervals account
  nominally for multiplicity within R20, not the entire adaptive research history.

- R19's largest primary effect is on a brittle abstention parser. Steering toward
  a phrase already recognized by that parser can raise scores without changing
  semantic correctness. Whole-answer F1 can reward an incorrect short answer.
  Unblinded examples reveal these failure modes but cannot estimate their overall
  share. No semantic rubric was retuned on test, and no blinded semantic replication
  had occurred at R19 completion. R20 adds a blinded evaluation with its own substantive
  validity limitations, above. The static control is not demonstrably worse overall, the live
  benefit gate loses quality against always-guided generation, and all within-domain
  routing intervals include zero. The 260-case fit, 200-case calibration, finite
  strength/predictor search, inherited source heads, one shuffle and post-start
  implementation/audit corrections further limit scope.
- R18's finite single-feature gate has highly uneven domain call rates, no primary
  routing interval excluding zero, and unfavorable Hotpot call selection. SQuAD
  uses an adapted natural-abstention metric on a public development subset with
  23 held-out article clusters; it is not an official hidden-test result. Short
  answers, shared authored templates, weak abstention, lexical scoring and unknown
  pretraining exposure limit scope. A supervised threshold is not a calibrated
  account of hidden reasoning. Source-attention monitoring has substantial prior
  art, and unimplemented related methods cannot support superiority claims.
- R17 tests one checkpoint, a finite twelve-policy grid and three short-pilot
  feature families, with policy and gate selection sharing development outcomes.
  Its primary guidance intervals include zero and the live gates always call.
  Budget replay is exploratory and does not measure actual call savings or latency;
  its strongest authored routing result does not transfer to the Hotpot subset.
  Missing-evidence accuracy and parser coverage remain poor, and whole-answer
  EM/F1 can change for formatting alone. No optimal insertion point, long-form
  adaptive reasoning, trained neural fusion or concurrent serving is demonstrated.
- R16 selects its policy on constrained authored tasks and transfers it without
  task-specific calibration. Its coordinate search is finite; the selected
  strength reaches the search ceiling. Dynamic refresh does not recompute
  old cache entries. Natural-answer parsing can undercount paraphrases, and
  the 200-question HotpotQA subset is filtered by length. Its factorial
  supplement reuses exposed contexts and has exploratory status.
- R10 combines Jev ranking and rejection and lacks a direct-prompt control. R13
  separates several policies, but its direct arm uses constrained final labels
  and is not unrestricted default chat. Its grammar-specific result cannot
  establish performance on open-ended reasoning.
- R14 uses one greedy label token and oracle-selected heads on short authored
  containment contexts; the motifs overlap across splits and the 50% UNKNOWN
  reference is strong. Lexical/prompt superiority is unresolved. Its internal
  steering during the final phase cannot be pooled with R13's different task and
  unassisted final phase to identify a generally best insertion point.
- R15 improves the authored short-chain task but selects the largest head count
  and strength in its finite grid; no optimum beyond that grid is established.
  The longer-chain challenge remains below constant UNKNOWN, with inconclusive
  superiority over shuffled guidance. Its post-start service-admission amendment
  limits pristine preregistration claims despite fixed selection and retained failures.
- Equal configured limits did not produce equal actual compute. More search and
  different stopping are potential explanations independent of scorer knowledge.
- Final correctness does not certify intermediate explanations. Most trace
  diagnostics are mechanical, and current qualitative readings are not blinded
  independent human annotations.
- Shared test benchmarks may have appeared in pretraining. Excluding previously
  used cases in this project does not establish training-data noncontamination.
- A small model, one checkpoint, limited task distributions and a hosted judge version limit
  generalization. Provider outputs can vary despite pinned requested identifiers.
- Private external traces limit public per-example reproduction. R13/R14/R15 authored
  fixtures improve public transparency but introduce template bias.
- Post-hoc inspection informed the redesign. The old test is exposed and cannot
  test the new hypothesis independently. Smaller or negative effects are reportable.
